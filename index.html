FAQ

Questions we hear often.

Is Sarthya a replacement for therapy? +
No — Sarthya is a supportive companion, not a clinical tool. For serious mental health concerns, we always encourage connecting with our human counselors or a licensed professional. Sarthya is best for everyday emotional support, reflection, and building healthier habits.
Is my data private and confidential? +
The current demo is designed to be low-friction and does not require account creation. As Sarthya grows, privacy, consent, and user control will remain central. Please avoid sharing highly sensitive personal details in the demo version.
How are counselors vetted? +
The therapist network is planned as the next phase. Counselors will be onboarded only after credential checks, clear service guidelines, and a safe support process are in place.
Can I use Sarthya anonymously? +
Yes. You can start conversations without creating an account. If you choose to sign up, your display name can be anything you're comfortable with — you don't need to share your real identity.
What languages does Sarthya support? +
Sarthya currently focuses on English and simple Hindi-style communication. More Indian languages can be added as user demand becomes clearer.
What if I'm in a crisis or emergency? +
If you are in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or someone near you right now. In India, you can also contact iCall at 9152987821 or Vandrevala Foundation at 1860-2662-345. Sarthya is not an emergency service.

Founder's story
Why I built Sarthyaa
✕

I never wanted to build another app that waits for someone to fall apart before offering help.

Through my years of working around students, counselling and education, I kept noticing the same thing: people are surrounded by advice, information and expectations, yet at some of the most confusing moments of their lives, they still feel like they have to figure everything out alone.

A student preparing for an exam may need more than another lecture.
Someone choosing a career may need more than a list of colleges.
A person going through a difficult relationship may not immediately need therapy—they may first need a safe place to speak.
A parent may need guidance without being judged.
An employee struggling quietly at work may need support long before it becomes a crisis.

And the systems around us usually treat all of these as separate problems.

I wanted Sarthyaa to connect them.

Technology can be available at 2 AM. A human can understand what technology sometimes cannot. A counsellor can provide direction. A therapist can step in when professional care is needed. And sometimes, simply having someone walk beside you is enough to help you find your own answer.

That is why I chose the idea of a Saarthi.

A saarthi does not fight your battle for you.
He does not decide your destination.
He does not become the hero of your story.

He stays beside you, helps you see the road more clearly, and guides you when the path becomes difficult.

That is what I want Sarthyaa to become.

Not another company trying to make people dependent on a platform, but an ecosystem that helps people become more capable of navigating their own lives.

If Sarthyaa succeeds, I don't want its greatest achievement to be the number of subscriptions it sells.

I want it to be the number of times a student felt less alone before an exam.
The number of people who asked for help before reaching a breaking point.
The number of families that communicated better.
The number of people who made a career decision with clarity rather than fear.
And the number of institutions that began treating emotional wellbeing as seriously as performance.

That is the change I want Sarthyaa to create.

Founder, Sarthyaa
